# Catalyst Lifecycle Management in Oil Refineries

## Overview

Catalysts are essential materials in modern petroleum refining, enabling chemical reactions that convert heavy crude oil into valuable light products. This document covers catalyst types, degradation mechanisms, lifecycle management, and economic optimization.

## Catalyst Fundamentals

### What is a Refinery Catalyst?

A **catalyst** is a substance that facilitates chemical reactions without being consumed in the process. Refinery catalysts:
- **Increase reaction rates**: Enable processing at practical temperatures/pressures
- **Improve selectivity**: Direct reactions toward desired products
- **Reduce energy consumption**: Lower operating temperatures and pressures

### Key Catalyst Properties

#### Surface Area
- Measured in square meters per gram (m²/g)
- Fresh catalyst: 150-300 m²/g (typical)
- Provides active sites for chemical reactions
- Degrades over time due to pore blockage and sintering

#### Pore Structure
- **Macropores**: >50 nm diameter, enable bulk diffusion
- **Mesopores**: 2-50 nm, primary reaction sites
- **Micropores**: <2 nm, molecular sieve properties

#### Active Sites
- Metal functions: Platinum, palladium, nickel, molybdenum
- Acid sites: Zeolites, alumina surfaces
- Bi-functional: Both metal and acid sites working together

## Major Refinery Catalysts

### 1. FCC Catalyst (Fluid Catalytic Cracking)

#### Composition
- **Base matrix**: Alumina-silica (70-80%)
- **Zeolite (Y-type)**: Active cracking component (20-30%)
- **Clay binder**: Kaolin (5-15%)
- **Additives**: ZSM-5 for octane, metals traps

#### Function
Crack large molecules into gasoline-range hydrocarbons at 900-1000°F.

#### Catalyst Cycle
- **Fresh catalyst**: Very active, may over-crack initially
- **Equilibrium catalyst**: Steady-state mix of fresh and aged (2-4 weeks to reach)
- **Spent catalyst**: Continuously withdrawn and replaced

#### Unique Characteristics
- **Circulating system**: Regenerated continuously in separate vessel
- **Short contact time**: 2-5 seconds in reactor
- **Rapid coke burn-off**: 650-750°C in regenerator
- **Continuous losses**: 0.5-2.0 pounds catalyst per barrel feed (attrition)
- **Daily addition**: 1-5 tons fresh catalyst to maintain equilibrium

#### Performance Degradation
Not tracked by calendar time but by:
- **Metals contamination**: Nickel and vanadium from feed poison active sites
- **Unit cell size (UCS)**: Decreases as zeolite structure collapses
- **Activity index**: Measured by laboratory micro-activity test (MAT)

### 2. Reformer Catalyst

#### Composition
- **Platinum**: 0.2-0.8 wt% (primary metal)
- **Rhenium or Tin**: 0.2-0.5 wt% (promoter metal)
- **Support**: Alumina or zeolite (chlorided for acidity)

#### Function
Restructure naphtha molecules to increase octane (aromatics formation) while producing hydrogen at 850-950°F and 100-500 psi.

#### Catalyst Cycle Types

**Semi-Regenerative**:
- Run: 6-24 months
- Shutdown for regeneration: 2-4 weeks
- Total life: 5-10 years with 3-5 regenerations

**Cyclic Regeneration**:
- Multiple reactors (typically 4)
- One reactor being regenerated at any time
- Continuous operation
- Regeneration cycle: 1-2 weeks per reactor

**Continuous Regeneration (CCR)**:
- Catalyst continuously withdrawn and regenerated
- Near-constant activity
- Highest octane yield
- Most expensive capital cost

#### Performance Degradation

**Fresh Catalyst** (0-3 months):
- Highest activity
- May require lower temperature initially
- Octane: 98-100 RON
- Hydrogen production: 2000+ SCF/bbl

**Mid-Cycle** (3-12 months):
- Stable performance
- Temperature gradually increased to maintain conversion
- Octane: 95-98 RON
- Hydrogen production: 1800-2000 SCF/bbl

**End-of-Run** (12-24 months):
- Reduced activity
- Operating at maximum temperature (950°F)
- Octane: 92-95 RON
- Hydrogen production: 1500-1800 SCF/bbl
- Regeneration or replacement required

#### Deactivation Mechanisms
1. **Coke formation**: Blocks pores and active sites
2. **Sintering**: Metal particles agglomerate at high temperature
3. **Chloride loss**: Reduces acidity of support
4. **Sulfur poisoning**: Temporary (removed during regeneration)
5. **Metal contamination**: Arsenic, lead (permanent)

### 3. Hydrocracker Catalyst

#### Composition (Two-Stage System)

**First Stage (Hydrogenation)**:
- **Metals**: Nickel-molybdenum or nickel-tungsten (10-25 wt%)
- **Support**: Amorphous alumina
- **Function**: Remove sulfur, nitrogen, metals; saturate aromatics

**Second Stage (Cracking)**:
- **Metals**: Platinum or palladium (0.5-1.5 wt%)
- **Support**: Zeolite Y (acidic)
- **Function**: Crack saturated molecules to lighter products

#### Catalyst Cycle
- **Run length**: 2-4 years
- **Regeneration**: In-situ or ex-situ every 2-4 years
- **Total life**: 8-12 years with 2-3 regenerations

#### Performance Degradation

**Fresh Catalyst** (0-6 months):
- Learning optimal conditions
- Maximum conversion (85-95%)
- Lowest operating temperature (650-700°F)
- High diesel yield

**Stable Period** (6-24 months):
- Consistent performance
- Temperature increase: 1-2°F per month
- Conversion maintained: 80-90%

**Late Life** (24-48 months):
- Temperature near limit (780-800°F)
- Conversion decline: 75-85%
- Consider regeneration or replacement

#### Deactivation Mechanisms
1. **Metal sulfide deposition**: Nickel, vanadium, iron from feed
2. **Nitrogen compound adsorption**: Permanent poisoning of acid sites
3. **Coking**: Pore blockage in cracking catalyst
4. **Sintering**: Metal crystallite growth reduces dispersion

### 4. Hydrotreater Catalyst

#### Composition
- **Metals**: Cobalt-molybdenum (CoMo) or nickel-molybdenum (NiMo)
- **Support**: Alumina (gamma phase)
- **Metal loading**: 15-25 wt% (as oxides)

#### Function
Remove sulfur, nitrogen, and metals via hydrogenation at 600-750°F and 300-800 psi H₂.

#### Catalyst Cycle
- **Run length**: 1-3 years (depending on feed quality)
- **Regeneration**: Not always economical (often just replaced)
- **Total life**: 1-4 years

#### Performance Degradation

**Fresh Catalyst** (0-3 months):
- Maximum desulfurization (>99.5% sulfur removal)
- Lowest temperature (620-650°F)
- Learning phase: optimize H₂/oil ratio

**Gradual Decline** (3-18 months):
- Temperature increase: 2-3°F per month
- Desulfurization maintained: >98%
- Monitor breakthrough trends

**End-of-Run** (18-36 months):
- Temperature at limit (750-780°F)
- Sulfur breakthrough: <95% removal
- Delta P increase: Pressure drop across bed
- Replacement or regeneration required

#### Deactivation Mechanisms
1. **Coking**: Especially with heavy feeds
2. **Metal deposition**: Nickel, vanadium, iron, sodium
3. **Sintering**: High temperatures collapse pore structure
4. **Sulfur poisoning**: Reversible if H₂S partial pressure controlled

## Catalyst Lifecycle Stages

The project models catalyst lifecycle using five standardized stages tracking performance degradation and operational implications.

### Stage 1: Fresh (0-90 days)

#### Characteristics
- **Activity**: 100% baseline (highest conversion)
- **Temperature**: Lowest required for target conversion
- **Selectivity**: May not be optimal initially (break-in period)
- **Operational challenges**: Finding optimal operating window

#### Typical Performance
| Unit Type | Fresh Performance |
|-----------|-------------------|
| FCC | 75-80% conversion, high coke make |
| Reformer | 100 RON, 2200 SCF H₂/bbl |
| Hydrocracker | 90-95% conversion at 670°F |
| Hydrotreater | >99.5% desulfur at 630°F |

#### Management Actions
- Conservative startup conditions
- Gradual temperature/rate increases
- Monitor product quality closely
- Establish baseline performance

### Stage 2: Early-Mid (91-180 days)

#### Characteristics
- **Activity**: 95-98% efficiency
- **Temperature**: Slight increase from fresh (5-10°F)
- **Selectivity**: Optimal operating window achieved
- **Operational challenges**: Minimal; stable performance

#### Typical Performance
| Unit Type | Early-Mid Performance |
|-----------|----------------------|
| FCC | 73-78% conversion, equilibrium |
| Reformer | 98-100 RON, 2000-2200 SCF H₂/bbl |
| Hydrocracker | 88-93% conversion at 680-690°F |
| Hydrotreater | >99% desulfur at 640-650°F |

#### Management Actions
- Maintain optimal conditions
- Routine monitoring
- Minor adjustments as needed

### Stage 3: Mid-Cycle (181-365 days)

#### Characteristics
- **Activity**: 90-95% efficiency
- **Temperature**: Moderate increase from fresh (15-25°F)
- **Selectivity**: Still good but declining
- **Operational challenges**: Monitor trends, plan for eventual decline

#### Typical Performance
| Unit Type | Mid-Cycle Performance |
|-----------|----------------------|
| FCC | 70-75% conversion (maintain with temp/catalyst) |
| Reformer | 95-98 RON, 1800-2000 SCF H₂/bbl at 880-900°F |
| Hydrocracker | 85-90% conversion at 700-720°F |
| Hydrotreater | 98-99% desulfur at 660-680°F |

#### Management Actions
- Increase temperature to maintain conversion
- Monitor for early signs of deactivation
- Begin planning regeneration or replacement timing
- Economic analysis: run longer vs. regenerate now

### Stage 4: Late-Mid (366-540 days)

#### Characteristics
- **Activity**: 85-90% efficiency
- **Temperature**: Significant increase (30-50°F above fresh)
- **Selectivity**: Noticeably reduced
- **Operational challenges**: Temperature approaching limits, yields declining

#### Typical Performance
| Unit Type | Late-Mid Performance |
|-----------|----------------------|
| FCC | 67-72% conversion (add fresh catalyst rate) |
| Reformer | 93-96 RON, 1600-1800 SCF H₂/bbl at 920-940°F |
| Hydrocracker | 80-87% conversion at 730-750°F |
| Hydrotreater | 96-98% desulfur at 700-720°F |

#### Management Actions
- Operating near temperature limits
- Plan definite regeneration/replacement date
- Coordinate with turnaround planning
- Begin procurement of replacement catalyst

### Stage 5: End-of-Run (541+ days)

#### Characteristics
- **Activity**: 75-85% efficiency
- **Temperature**: At or near maximum design limit
- **Selectivity**: Poor; undesired byproducts increasing
- **Operational challenges**: Catalyst failure imminent, product quality at risk

#### Typical Performance
| Unit Type | End-of-Run Performance |
|-----------|------------------------|
| FCC | <67% conversion; high slurry yield |
| Reformer | <93 RON, <1600 SCF H₂/bbl at 950°F limit |
| Hydrocracker | <80% conversion at 770-780°F limit |
| Hydrotreater | <96% desulfur; breakthrough risk |

#### Management Actions
- **Immediate**: Schedule shutdown for regeneration/replacement
- Reduce throughput if necessary to maintain quality
- Prepare for startup with fresh catalyst
- Document deactivation for future cycle planning

## Catalyst Regeneration

### Purpose
Restore catalyst activity by removing coke deposits and reconstituting active sites.

### Regeneration Process

#### Step 1: Cooling and Purging
- Cool unit to safe temperature (typically <400°F)
- Purge hydrocarbons with nitrogen

#### Step 2: Controlled Combustion
- Introduce dilute air (1-2% O₂ initially)
- Burn off carbonaceous deposits (coke)
- Control exotherm: Max temperature 950-1000°F
- Duration: 3-7 days depending on coke level

#### Step 3: Oxychlorination (Reformer Only)
- Restore chloride levels on support
- Re-disperse platinum particles
- Duration: 1-2 days

#### Step 4: Reduction
- Convert metal oxides back to active metal state
- Use hydrogen at gradually increasing temperature
- Duration: 2-4 days

#### Step 5: Startup
- Load feed gradually
- Monitor for hot spots
- Achieve stable operation: 1-3 days

### Regeneration Economics

**Typical Costs** (per regeneration):
- Reformer: $500,000 - $2,000,000
- Hydrocracker: $1,000,000 - $3,000,000
- Hydrotreater: $300,000 - $1,000,000

**Activity Recovery**:
- First regeneration: 90-95% of fresh activity
- Second regeneration: 85-90% of fresh activity
- Third regeneration: 80-85% of fresh activity
- Diminishing returns after 3-4 regenerations

## Economic Optimization

### When to Regenerate vs. Continue Running?

#### Cost of Continuing Operation
```
Lost Margin = (Reduced Yield × Product Value) + (Increased Energy × Energy Cost)

Example: Reformer at end-of-run
- Octane loss: 2 units (98 → 96 RON)
- Hydrogen loss: 400 SCF/bbl (2000 → 1600 SCF/bbl)
- Energy cost: +5% natural gas
- Lost margin: $1-2 million per month for 40,000 BPD unit
```

#### Cost of Regeneration
```
Total Cost = Direct Costs + Opportunity Cost

Direct Costs:
- Labor and materials: $500,000 - $2,000,000
- Duration: 2-4 weeks

Opportunity Cost:
- Lost production: 40,000 BPD × 28 days × $5/bbl margin = $5.6 million
- Consider market timing (schedule in low-margin periods)
```

#### Decision Framework
```
If (Lost Margin per Month × Months until planned turnaround) > Total Regeneration Cost:
    Regenerate now
Else:
    Continue running and regenerate during planned turnaround
```

### Catalyst Purchase Optimization

#### Factors to Consider
1. **Price**: Fresh catalyst cost ($10-50/lb depending on metals)
2. **Expected life**: Longer life justifies higher price
3. **Regeneration capability**: More regenerations = lower lifecycle cost
4. **Performance**: Higher activity/selectivity = better yields

#### Lifecycle Cost Analysis
```
Lifecycle Cost per Barrel = 
    (Purchase Price + Regeneration Costs - Salvage Value) / Total Barrels Processed

Example: Reformer catalyst
- Purchase: $5 million (Pt-Re on alumina, 40,000 lb)
- Life: 3 years with 2 regenerations
- Salvage (Pt recovery): $1 million
- Regeneration: $1 million each × 2 = $2 million
- Total barrels: 40,000 BPD × 1095 days = 43.8 million bbls

Lifecycle cost = ($5M + $2M - $1M) / 43.8M = $0.137 per barrel
```

## Catalyst Performance Metrics

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

#### Activity Metrics
- **Conversion**: % of feed converted to products
- **Selectivity**: % of desired product in total products
- **Yield**: Barrels of product per barrel of feed

#### Aging Metrics
- **Temperature required**: Track increase over time (°F/month)
- **Pressure drop**: Indicates pore plugging (psi increase/month)
- **Metals on catalyst**: Ni+V ppm (for hydroprocessing)

#### Economic Metrics
- **Cost per barrel**: Catalyst cost allocated per barrel processed
- **Lost margin**: Value lost due to reduced performance
- **Run length**: Days between regenerations/replacements

### Monitoring and Trending

#### Daily Monitoring
- Operating temperature
- Conversion/yield
- Product quality (octane, sulfur, etc.)
- Pressure drop

#### Weekly Trending
- Temperature vs. time (aging rate)
- Product quality trends
- Yield structure changes

#### Monthly Analysis
- Catalyst age vs. performance correlation
- Lost margin calculation
- Regeneration timing planning

#### Catalyst Sampling (Quarterly)
- Metals analysis
- Surface area measurement
- Carbon content
- Activity testing (lab scale)
